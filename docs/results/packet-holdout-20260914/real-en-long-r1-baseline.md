# Question: Produce a cautious evidence-backed briefing on the pilot's measured outcomes and its unresolved limits.

## Answer

The pilot established that 71% of 4,860 included visits reached a desk within the displayed wait band, with results ranging from 66% to 75% across three branches [S1][S2]. It did not establish that the display improved service relative to no display, reduced abandonment, worked equally well for all groups, or would produce similar citywide results [S1][S2]. The evidence supports further controlled and accessibility testing, but not citywide procurement or deployment at this stage [S1][S2]. (judgment)

## Evidence

The pilot ran from 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15 in three municipal library branches [S1]. Its 4,860 included visits excluded appointments, school groups, and days when the ticket printer was offline [S1][S2].

Across those visits, 71% reached a desk within the displayed wait band, and the reported branch range was 66% to 75% [S1][S2]. The display communicated a band rather than an exact wait in minutes [S1].

The 71% result describes agreement between the displayed band and recorded desk time, not universal accuracy or improvement caused by the display [S1][S2]. Differences among branch configurations likewise cannot be interpreted causally because the results were unadjusted and no causal experiment was conducted [S1][S2].

Staff recorded 58 display corrections: 21 after a sick call, 17 after a late-opening program, 12 after a ticket-printer restart, and 8 with only a short free-text description [S1][S2]. These staff-entered classifications were not independently audited [S2].

An accessibility review of 18 volunteer sessions found that 15 announced the band through the configured screen reader [S2]. Two sessions involved browser-extension conditions, and one involved a changed display language without a translated label [S2].

The materials contain no pre-display comparison, visitor randomization, comparison of alternative displays, or estimate of staffing-demand effects [S1][S2]. They also do not establish that queue abandonment declined [S1][S2].

The appendix provides anonymized counts but not individual visitor demographics [S1]. Consequently, the evidence cannot establish equal usefulness across visitor groups [S1][S2].

Citywide replacement would require a separate budget decision [S2]. The materials contain no contract, signed vendor selection, purchase order, rollout calendar, approved citywide benefit estimate, or deployment authorization [S2].

The pilot can inform later deliberation, but its match rate neither predicts citywide savings nor authorizes procurement [S1][S2]. The supplied materials do not approve a rollout date or citywide deployment [S2].

## Counter-evidence and limits

No source in the supplied record directly contradicts the reported pilot measurements [S1][S2]. However, S2 is truncated, so omitted portions could not be reviewed here [S2]. The accessibility sessions were simulated, reviewer-observed, and recruited through a local advisory group rather than designed as representative population testing [S2]. The supplied excerpts do not measure whether visitors abandoned the queue less often [S1]. These omissions limit causal, equity, accessibility, and economic conclusions without negating the observed match rate [S1][S2].

## Next actions

1. Run a pre-specified follow-up that includes a comparison condition and measures abandonment, staffing demand, and visitor understanding [S1][S2]. (judgment)
2. Test translated labels across representative devices, languages, browser settings, and assistive technologies while retaining a staffed alternative during evaluation [S2]. (judgment)
3. Define an outcome threshold and require accessibility, operations, budget, and projected-benefit reviews before any procurement decision [S2]. (judgment)
