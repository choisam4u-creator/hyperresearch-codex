## Evidence

The municipal library tested its queue display in three branches from 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15. [S1]

Across 4,860 included visits, 71% reached a desk within the displayed wait band, while the reported results for individual branches ranged from 66% to 75%. [S1][S2]

The 71% result does not mean every estimate was correct, and the records neither compare performance with a pre-display period nor show whether queue abandonment declined. [S1][S2]

Staff recorded 58 display corrections: 21 after a sick call, 17 after a late-opening program, 12 after a ticket-printer restart, and eight with only a short free-text description; these staff-entered labels were not independently audited. [S1][S2]

The accessibility review covered 18 volunteer sessions and observed screen-reader announcement of the wait band in 15; two other sessions involved a browser extension suppressing the live update, and one involved a changed display language without a translated label. [S2]

Those observations demonstrate successful announcements and particular barriers within the reviewed setup, but they provide neither a population accessibility rate nor evidence that the display fails in every setting. [S2]

Because the retained data excluded individual visitor demographics, the materials cannot establish whether the display was equally useful across visitor groups. [S1][S2]

The pilot did not randomize visitors, compare alternative display designs, estimate effects on staffing demand, or adjust branch results for hour, service type, or staff roster. [S2]

The materials contain no contract, rollout date, citywide benefit estimate, or rollout approval, and they state that citywide replacement would require a separate budget decision. [S2]
