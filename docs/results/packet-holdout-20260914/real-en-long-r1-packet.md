# Question: Produce a cautious evidence-backed briefing on the pilot's measured outcomes and its unresolved limits.

## Answer

The pilot produced a useful but narrow operational result: 71% of 4,860 included visits reached a desk within the displayed wait band, with branch results ranging from 66% to 75%.[S1][S2] This measures agreement between displayed bands and recorded desk times, not reduced abandonment, causal effectiveness, accessibility for the wider population, or equitable outcomes.[S1][S2] The evidence supports further controlled testing, but it does not support citywide rollout because procurement, benefits, budget, and deployment remain undecided.[S2] A defensible decision criterion is whether a follow-up can reproduce acceptable matching while measuring abandonment, accessibility, and subgroup outcomes under a pre-specified protocol. ## Evidence

The municipal library tested the queue display at three branches from 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15, showing estimated wait bands instead of exact-minute predictions.[S1]

Among 4,860 included visits, 71% reached a desk within the displayed band, while individual branch results ranged from 66% to 75%.[S1][S2]

The calculation excluded appointments, school groups, and ticket-printer-offline days; a transferred ticket was also excluded when transfer occurred before its first desk interaction.[S1][S2]

Staff logged 58 corrections: 21 after a sick call, 17 after a late-opening program, 12 after a ticket-printer restart, and eight with only a short free-text description; staff entered these labels, and they were not independently audited.[S1][S2]

The materials cannot establish that the display reduced abandonment because they lack a pre-display comparison and do not report how many visitors left before taking tickets or whether that behavior changed.[S1][S2]

The accessibility review covered 18 volunteer sessions in July, of which 15 produced a screen-reader announcement; two failures involved a browser extension suppressing the live update, and one involved a changed display language without a translated label.[S2]

Those observations identify specific successes and barriers in the tested setup, but they are not a population rate because the review did not cover all devices, languages, assistive technologies, or unassisted visitors.[S2]

The absence of individual demographics and visitor-level characteristics prevents a supported conclusion that the display was equally useful across visitor groups.[S1][S2]

The unadjusted branch range does not demonstrate that one display configuration caused better matching, because hour, service type, and staff roster were not controlled.[S2]

The materials contain no contract, selected vendor, rollout date, citywide benefit estimate, or citywide approval, and replacement across all branches would require a separate budget decision.[S2]

## Counter-evidence and limits

The supplied sources do not directly disagree, but they repeatedly constrain interpretation of the headline match rate.[S1][S2] In particular, 71% agreement does not mean every estimate was correct, and the observational protocol cannot isolate the display’s causal effect.[S1][S2] The branch comparison is confounded by operating differences, while the small accessibility exercise cannot be generalized beyond its volunteer sessions.[S2] Equality across visitor groups, changes in abandonment, benefits from clearer or translated notices, and the effect of retaining a staffed alternative could not be verified.[S1][S2] S2 is marked as truncated, so this briefing cannot confirm whether omitted portions contain additional relevant detail.
