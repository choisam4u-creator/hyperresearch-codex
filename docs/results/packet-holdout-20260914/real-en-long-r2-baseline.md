# Question: Produce a cautious evidence-backed briefing on the pilot's measured outcomes and its unresolved limits.

## Answer

The pilot produced a useful but limited operational result: 71% of 4,860 included visits reached a desk within the displayed wait band, with branch results ranging from 66% to 75%. [S1][S2] This finding describes performance among included visits at three branches; it does not show that the display reduced abandonment, outperformed having no display, or would deliver the same result citywide. [S1][S2] A follow-up should be judged by pre-specified matching, accessibility, abandonment, and outage-recording criteria while retaining a staffed alternative. ## Evidence

From 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15, the municipal library tested a queue display at three branches, showing an estimated wait band rather than an exact waiting time. [S1]

Across 4,860 included visits, 71% reached a desk within the displayed band, while individual branch results ranged from 66% to 75%. [S1][S2]

The analysis excluded appointments, school groups, and days when the ticket printer was offline, so the reported rate applies only to the defined included visits. [S1][S2]

Staff logged 58 display corrections after schedule changes or unexpected absences. [S1] [S1][S2]

During 18 volunteer accessibility sessions in July, screen readers announced the wait band in 15 sessions; two failures involved an extension suppressing the live update, and one involved a changed display language without a translated label. [S2]

The 71% matching result does not establish universal estimate accuracy, reduced abandonment, or superiority to no display because the pilot had neither a before period nor a comparison group. [S1][S2]

The records do not report how many visitors left without tickets, whether abandonment changed during the pilot, or why visitors left, preventing a supported claim that abandonment declined. [S2]

Branch comparisons cannot identify a superior configuration because North used three bands, Central used four on weekday evenings, East temporarily merged two bands during a desk closure, and results were not adjusted for hour, service type, or staffing roster. [S2]

The accessibility sessions demonstrate successful announcements and specific barriers only in the tested volunteer setup; they do not estimate population accessibility across all devices, languages, assistive technologies, or unassisted visitors. [S2]

Because the retained records contain no visitor demographics, the evidence cannot establish whether the display was equally useful across visitor groups. [S1][S2]

The three-branch results should not be treated as a citywide deployment estimate. [S1] [S1][S2]

Reviewers recommended retaining a staffed alternative and testing translated labels, while the steering note proposed a limited follow-up that would also pre-specify ticket-printer outage records; these remain proposals rather than measured effects or deployment approval. [S2]

## Counter-evidence and limits

No source directly contradicts the reported counts or match rate, but the absence of a control or before period prevents causal interpretation. [S1][S2] Visitors who left without tickets are unmeasured, and the records do not show whether visitors understood the displayed band before joining the queue. [S2] Every participating branch retained a staffed desk, but the files do not establish its use, effect on confusion, comparative staffing cost, or permanent policy owner. [S2] [S2] Source S2 is truncated, so omitted material could not be independently assessed from the supplied note. [S2]

## Next actions

1. Conduct the proposed limited follow-up with translated labels, a retained staffed alternative, and pre-specified ticket-printer outage records. [S2]
2. Add a before period or comparison group and record abandonment counts and reasons so later analysis can assess effects rather than matching alone. [S1][S2] (judgment)
3. Test more devices, languages, assistive technologies, and unassisted visitor journeys before making a population-level accessibility claim. [S2] (judgment)
4. Defer citywide procurement until accessibility and operations review, benefit analysis, funding, vendor selection, and formal approval are complete. [S1][S2] (judgment)
