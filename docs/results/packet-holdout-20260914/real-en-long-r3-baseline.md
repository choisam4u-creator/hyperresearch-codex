# Question: Produce a cautious evidence-backed briefing on the pilot's measured outcomes and its unresolved limits.

## Answer

The pilot provides a credible descriptive result: 71% of 4,860 included visits reached a desk within the displayed wait band across three branches.[S1][S2] It does not establish that the display improved waiting, reduced abandonment, lowered staffing demand, or would perform similarly citywide.[S1][S2] A follow-up should distinguish descriptive accuracy from visitor benefit and test accessibility under broader real-world conditions.[S1][S2] Citywide procurement should remain contingent on stronger comparative, accessibility, and operational evidence.[S2] (judgment)

| Decision dimension | Measured outcome | Conditions and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Wait-band matching | 71% overall; branch range 66%–75%.[S1][S2] | Appointments, school groups, printer-offline days, and qualifying specialist transfers were excluded.[S1][S2] |
| Operational stability | 58 display corrections were logged.[S1][S2] | Corrections followed sick calls, late-opening programs, printer restarts, or briefly described events.[S1][S2] |
| Screen-reader access | The band was announced in 15 of 18 observed volunteer sessions.[S2] | Two extension cases suppressed the update, one translated label was missing, and broader device and language performance is unknown.[S2] |
| Citywide suitability | No citywide outcome was measured.[S1][S2] | No deployment authorization, selected vendor, contract, rollout date, or citywide benefit estimate was provided.[S2] |

## Evidence

S1 reports that the pilot ran in three branches from 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15 and displayed a wait band rather than an exact minute.[S1]

S1 reports that 71% of 4,860 included visits matched the displayed band; S2 reports a 66%–75% unadjusted branch range.[S1][S2]

The matching calculation excluded appointments, school groups, days when ticket printers were offline, and tickets transferred to a specialist before their first desk interaction.[S1][S2]

S1 reports 58 corrections, concentrated in two branches during the first two weeks, following schedule changes or unexpected absences.[S1]

The July accessibility review covered 18 volunteer sessions, of which 15 successfully announced the band through the configured screen reader.[S2] Two sessions encountered browser-extension suppression of the live update, and one encountered a changed display language without a translated label.[S2]

The available evidence shows no pre-display comparison and no measurement of whether visitors abandoned the queue less often.[S1]

Because only anonymized totals and no individual visitor demographics were retained, the evidence cannot show whether the display was equally useful across visitor groups.[S1][S2]

The branch range was not adjusted for time of day, service type, or staff roster, so differences cannot be attributed to a branch’s display configuration.[S2]

S2 leaves the budget source and deployment decision open and contains no approval for a citywide rollout.[S2]

Results from only the three participating branches should not be interpreted as an estimate of citywide performance.[S1]

## Counter-evidence and limits

No source disputes the recorded 71% match rate, but both sources sharply limit its interpretation.[S1][S2] One meeting attendee proposed treating the pilot as proof that every library should use the display, but the meeting chair revised the wording to state that the evidence only informs a decision and requires more testing.[S2] The materials do not count people who left before receiving tickets or provide reasons for every abandoned ticket, leaving abandonment effects unresolved.[S1][S2] They also do not establish visitor understanding before queue entry, population-level accessibility, equity across demographic groups, or performance across all devices, languages, and assistive technologies.[S1][S2] Because S2 is truncated, its unavailable portions could not be independently assessed here.[S2] S2 leaves the budget source and deployment decision open and gives no date for a citywide rollout.[S2]

## Next actions

1. Run the proposed limited follow-up with translated labels, a staffed alternative, and pre-specified recording of ticket-printer outages.[S2]
2. Add a before-period or controlled comparison and define outcome thresholds before data collection.[S1][S2] (judgment)
3. Measure abandonment, visitor understanding, staffing demand, and accessibility across additional devices, languages, assistive technologies, and visitor groups.[S1][S2] (judgment)
4. Defer procurement until the follow-up evidence and separate accessibility, operations, and budget reviews are complete.[S2] (judgment)
